The leverage frontier — pick your point (existing book, 10.5% MTF)
1.0× uses your own capital only (no borrowing). 1.3–1.6× is the return sweet spot at a drawdown you can size to. The Donchian stop + buffer keep the Calmar high (1.35–1.48) even under leverage — far above the plain top-8 book's ~1.1.
Borrow-rate sensitivity — 8% (futures basis) vs 10.5% (MTF)
Financing costs little because leverage is only on ~75% of months (gate-on) and only on the borrowed slice — 10.5% vs 8% MTF costs just 0.1–1.1% CAGR. Stock-futures financing (~8%) needs all 8 rotating names to have liquid F&O every month, which mid-caps often lack — so MTF is the realistic assumption.
Why vol-targeting does NOT help (tested on the momentum family; figures from the plain top-8 variant — the lesson transfers)
Dynamic leverage from trailing vol lands below the static frontier at every matched average leverage. Two reasons: (1) the index gate already handles the downside, so vol-targeting duplicates it with lag + cost; (2) momentum's biggest up-years (2014 +108%, 2021 +101%) are high-vol rallies — vol-targeting de-levers straight into the melt-ups, amputating the fat right tail that is the edge.
Takeaway
The index gate makes leverage survivable; the Donchian stop + buffer keep it efficient. On the existing live book, static 1.3–1.6× leverage → 40.4–47.9% CAGR at −28 to −35% drawdown, Calmar ~1.4, zero margin calls across 2006–2026 (incl. 2008 & 2020). At 1.0× (own capital, no borrowing) it's 32.7% CAGR / −22% DD vs NIFTYBEES buy-hold's 11.7% / −60%. This book levers far better than the plain top-8 version — its Calmar holds ~1.35–1.48 across all leverage (vs the plain book's decay toward ~0.9) because the per-stock stop caps the drawdowns leverage would otherwise amplify. Leverage is a proportional trade of drawdown for return — not a free lunch, but a well-controlled one. Vol-targeting and deeper concentration (N<8) both reduce risk-adjusted return. This is the same momentum edge magnified, not new alpha.
