Weekly SuperTrend (10,3): a YouTube system, stress-tested to the ground

Buy on green, exit on red, size 5–7%, book 40/40/20, five "hacks". We tested every claim on Nifty 50 / 200 / Midcap 150 / Smallcap 250 / 500, net of cost & tax, 2010–2026.
Per-name timing: NO EDGE Index crash-overlay: doubles Calmar ETF timing: halves drawdown 40/40/20 & regime gate: HURT

The one-paragraph verdict. As pitched — timing each stock in and out on its SuperTrend — the system loses to simply owning the same names (the eye-catching "beats the index" was survivorship + breadth, not the signal). But the indicator has a real, robust use the video never mentions: applied at the market level — one SuperTrend on the index, controlling exposure to the whole book — it cuts drawdown by more than half for a small return give-up. The cleanest, most tradeable form is to trend-time the index ETF itself: on NIFTYBEES, net-of-tax you give up ~1.5pp CAGR (10.6 → 9.0%) and cut max drawdown from −36% to −14%, roughly doubling Calmar and Sharpe. It's real, it's clean, it's infinitely scalable — and it's a well-known tactical-timing result, not a discovery.

Hold NIFTYBEES when its daily ST(7,3) is green; on red, sell and park in a liquid fund (~4.5% net, after its expense + slab tax); re-enter on the next green. Realistic — net of cost, the liquid-fund net return, and T+1 settlement lag:

Keep NIFTYBEES (never sold → no equity CGT, deferred like B&H; no T+1 lag; margin funded by pledging the ETF) and short NIFTY futures on the red signal. The short side is a structural loser. During ST-red periods the index still RISES — +6%/yr on daily ST, +19%/yr on weekly ST — because the slow filter flags red after the drop and stays red through the recovery, so you short into the bounce. Markets drift up and spend more time recovering than crashing. Short-only earns ~nothing (daily +0.8%) or loses (weekly −1.9%) at huge drawdown, and going bidirectional cuts CAGR and roughly DOUBLES the drawdown (worse than buy-and-hold). Weekly is worse than daily throughout — too slow. Stay long-only: hold the ETF, hedge to flat in downtrends, never short.

And the flashy 46.5% "best CAGR" is itself the lower-cap capacity/survivorship mirage (research/62).

08Honest caveats

  • Survivorship bias is the root of the illusion — today's index members applied to the past; the returns are carried by names that are in the index because they 10–50×'d.
  • The DD-halving is a well-known result (Faber-style tactical index timing), cleanly confirmed on Indian ETFs. Value = clean + scalable + simple, not novelty.
  • Tax is the entire give-up on the ETF version (~5 switches/yr realise STCG). A futures/puts hedge recovers most of it.
  • Not universal — it halves DD on trending, crash-prone equity indices; it hurt GOLDBEES.
  • Regime-dependent & whipsaws in choppy years (2025–26 lagged). Pre-tax "zero give-up" is period-flattered by sharp, recoverable crashes (2011, 2020) the filter dodged.
