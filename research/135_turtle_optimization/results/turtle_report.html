The Turtle rules, scored one at a time

All five rules of the classic Dennis/Covel breakout system, tested on 78 Indian F&O names over 21 years. Three of the five rules destroy value. The system's fame rests on the two that survive.

2006-01 → 2026-08 78 symbols ~100 book configs net of costs long only

Verdict · concluded · no deploy

The optimization is real. The book still isn't.

Stripping the system down to its two good rules lifts Calmar from 0.02 to 0.53 — a genuine, large improvement. But out-of-sample 2024–2026 is negative for every Turtle variant tested, against a benchmark that made +5.3%. The edge is real historically and gone lately.

The momentum book already running beats every Turtle variant in every era — 31.8% CAGR against 16.0%, at the same drawdown. Recommendation: don't build this.

Correction, 30 Aug. The first version of this page showed the momentum book at 12.6% CAGR — roughly benchmark — which Arun correctly challenged against its published 31.9%. That arm was wrong three ways: it ranked momentum inside the 78 F&O large caps instead of the top-250 the book actually trades, it bolted a daily stop from the live book onto research/75's monthly rebalance rules, and a re-entry defect left it sitting in cash after every exit until the next month-end. This version drives research/75's own engine, reproducing its published number to within 0.1pt (31.78% vs 31.9%). The correction strengthens the conclusion rather than weakening it — momentum's true margin over the Turtle is far wider than first reported.

The scorecard

Which rules earn their place

Every rule was tested against the same universe, period and cost model, holding the others fixed. The verdicts below are what the data says about each rule independently.

Rule 1
20/55-day breakout entry

The original System-1 setting is genuinely the best of 11 channel lengths tested. The Turtles picked well.

Keep · 20
Rule 2
N-based position sizing (1% risk)

Equal-notional beats volatility-keyed sizing at every risk level: Calmar 1.06 vs 0.83 / 0.84 / 0.70. This is the fourth independent time vol-sizing has lost in this research programme.

Reject
Rule 3
2N hard stop loss

The single most damaging rule in the system. Removing it improves return and drawdown, monotonically, in 10 of 11 channels.

Reject
Rule 4
Pyramiding to 4 units every ½N

Coupled to the stop, it is ruinous: mean Calmar falls 0.47 → 0.40 → 0.24 → 0.08 as units go 1 → 4. Each add ratchets the shared stop up under the newest unit, so the position is largest exactly when its stop is tightest.

Reject
Rule 5
10/20-day opposite-channel exit

The trailing channel is the exit that works, and tight beats loose at every entry length. This rule carries the entire edge.

Keep · 10

Rule 3 in detail

The stop is what breaks it

Averaged across all 11 entry/exit channel combinations, risk-adjusted return rises monotonically as the hard stop is loosened — and is best with no hard stop at all. Drawdown improves too, which is the counter-intuitive part: the 2N stop was adding drawdown, not removing it.

Why it happens. A trend book already has a loss limiter — the trailing channel exit. A second, tighter limiter mostly ejects positions that were about to recover, and the trades it saves you from are smaller than the winners it costs you.

A near miss

The trap the plateau test caught

Pyramiding produced the single best in-sample number in the entire study — Calmar 1.06, against 0.84 without it. Picking on that number alone would have been the obvious move.

The rule declared before the study started was that a winner must have strong neighbours, not just a strong score. Pyramiding failed it: of six channel lengths, it improved one and degraded five.

Borrowed machinery

The momentum gate and the put overlay

Two pieces of the live momentum book were ported onto the Turtle book: its 100-SMA regime gate, and its protective-put overlay — at a risk-off signal, hold the stocks and buy NIFTY puts instead of selling. Neither transplant helps enough to matter.

The gate has no stable answer

In-sample the 200-day gate wins (Calmar 1.06 vs 0.72 for the 100-SMA vs 0.89 ungated). In validation the order reverses — 100-SMA 0.21, ungated 0.16, 200-day 0.08. Whichever gate looks best depends on the era you fit it in, so the in-sample ranking shouldn't be read as a finding.

Puts don't rescue the book

At the momentum book's own specification — at-the-money, 2× equity notional, ~14 DTE, rolled — no hedged variant beats simply gating to cash in-sample (best hedge 0.93 against 1.06 for the plain gate). In validation the hedged arms edge ahead (0.26 vs 0.21), but by less than the gate choice itself moves things.

That matches what the live momentum book already concluded: its hedge is switched off, with the note that the cash-exit gate beat it over the full cycle.

One clean, stable sub-finding: 5%-out-of-the-money puts beat at-the-money in every pairing tested — both eras, both spread assumptions. Cheaper convexity buys more protection per rupee. That's worth carrying into any future hedge design.

Caveats

What would change this answer

  • Survivorship. The universe is today's F&O list projected back to 2005. In-sample returns are inflated by construction — another reason the in-sample-to-out-of-sample gap is partly bias, not only decay.
  • A bug was found and fixed mid-study. The first put-overlay run expensed the full premium and marked the option's decay from that same level, double-counting roughly 1.8% of NAV per roll and producing absurd −99.9% results. A hand-check (11.8%/yr expected drag versus 84%/yr reported) caught it. All hedge numbers here are post-fix; the invalid output is kept in the folder, renamed, rather than deleted.
  • Option pricing before 2015 is modelled. INDIAVIX only starts in 2015; earlier implied vol is realised vol scaled by a premium factor calibrated on the 2,884-day overlap (k = 1.331). Pre-2015 hedge figures carry that assumption.
  • Roughly 100 configurations were scored. Controls were the pre-declared plateau requirement, a single out-of-sample consumption, and per-year reporting throughout.
  • Shorts were never re-tested — three prior studies closed directional equity shorts permanently, so this was long-only throughout.

What to keep

Four lessons that outlive the study

  • Hard stops on multi-week equity trend books cost return and add drawdown. The trailing exit is the stop.
  • Pyramiding tied to a ratcheting stop is actively destructive — the two rules are individually questionable and jointly ruinous.
  • Volatility-keyed sizing lost to equal-notional for the fourth time. Treat equal-notional as settled and stop re-testing it.
  • 5%-OTM index puts dominate at-the-money for portfolio hedging, consistently.
